# Experimental WebRTC video mirroring

WebRTC uses a custom receiver and Android's normal screen-capture consent. Full-display sharing may follow app switches; selected-app sharing stays within the platform selection. Source changes cannot update a hosted receiver: a compatible receiver must be deployed and registered separately.

WebRTC sends video plus, by default, playback-capture audio. WebRTC audio is playback capture, not the microphone: media and game audio from apps that allow audio capture (NOT limited to the shared app, see below). It needs `RECORD_AUDIO` only because Android's `AudioRecord` requires it even for playback capture. Apps that opt out of capture, DRM-protected audio and audio tagged `USAGE_UNKNOWN` are not captured. The phone keeps playing sound locally (capture is a copy at fixed volume). The microphone is used only if you turn on the opt-in "Mix in microphone" switch (Android 11 or newer, default off, locked while casting; see below). If the permission is denied, the OS is older than Android 10, or any part of the setup fails, the cast proceeds video-only. WebRTC media is encrypted with DTLS-SRTP (the app sets no option that disables encryption), but the receiver is not authenticated: the Cast control channel accepts any certificate and carries the DTLS fingerprint, so a LAN attacker who impersonates the receiver could receive the stream. HLS (not WebRTC) is plaintext HTTP. With the mix off (default) no microphone recorder is created or started by this app's code. With it on, the app records the microphone with a separate recorder outside the libwebrtc audio module; the library itself is still configured with audio source REMOTE_SUBMIX so it cannot build a microphone recorder by itself (if it tried, creation would fail and a guard disables audio, which also stops the separate microphone recorder). Mix on: Android 12 and newer show a microphone indicator; the foreground service then also declares the microphone type and its notification says "Microphone is mixed in". There is no echo cancellation: if the TV speaker is audible near the phone you will hear echo (headphones on the phone do not prevent it); the mix lowers the mic level, gates quiet sound, and mutes the mic mix if a sustained loud level suggests feedback. Details and device checks: [docs/mic-mix-TDD.md](docs/mic-mix-TDD.md). This rests on a bytecode review of the pinned library plus one device observation, not a guarantee. Device-verified on one phone and TV (Pixel 10 Pro Fold, Chromecast, Android 17): audio capture without the microphone, and picture plus sound both arriving on the TV once with Chrome shared. Audio/video sync and latency are NOT verified; background and locked-phone capture on Android 14-17 is unverified. The Settings toggle is `Audio (playback capture)`; when the permission is missing Settings explains it and offers a button (or an Open app settings button after a permanent denial). The library can create a default audio device module even when no local audio track is added.

**Sound from any app that allows audio capture is sent to the TV, even if you share only one app** (verified on a device: sharing Maps sent a movie playing in Chrome). Anything playing media or game audio on the phone can be heard on the TV.

How it works: the audio device module (ADM) is built with source REMOTE_SUBMIX. Under one lock the app disables ADM recording, lets the library allocate its buffers (creating no recorder), installs an `AudioPlaybackCapture` `AudioRecord` built from the cast's own `MediaProjection`, and re-enables recording. It first requires that the library has not started recording. A guard disables ADM recording on any error, stop or unexpected record, after which the capture thread releases the record within about 10 ms and the cast continues with video (the audio track then carries silence). On teardown the app disables recording and waits up to 300 ms (60 ms on the main thread) for the record to stop before stopping the projection; this is unverified on device. When the stop comes from the system capture chip, the platform kills the capture track before the app's projection callback runs, so `AudioRecord createRecord_l ... -22` and `Error -22 during AudioRecord native read` may be logged; this is expected and harmless. Audio has some delay relative to the phone speaker.


For an established capture, Stop synchronously closes the local peer/capture and control transport without waiting for BYE delivery. If a synchronous native build or capture-setup call is still in flight, terminal shutdown closes transport and cancels session work immediately, then disposes the peer once that call returns, including resources allocated after the shutdown callback. No lifecycle monitor is held across native calls or disposal; setup that blocks indefinitely also delays peer disposal. Connect, launch, offer, answer, ICE and heartbeat failures follow terminal cleanup; errors remain visible while service wake-lock and foreground ownership are released. A new attempt requests fresh platform consent.

WebRTC respects system routing. It does not bind the process to Wi-Fi or deliberately bind native sockets through the Android network monitor. VPN/firewall policy can block LAN access; change policy yourself only if desired. Native routing must still be checked on physical devices.

Tests run the production session and service with injected transport/native peer boundaries under Robolectric. They establish lifecycle ownership at those boundaries, not libwebrtc encoder behavior, microphone indicators, actual MediaProjection teardown, Chromecast compatibility or performance. Physical latency remains unmeasured.

Routing implementation reference: [WebRTC Java options](https://webrtc.googlesource.com/src/+/refs/heads/main/sdk/android/api/org/webrtc/PeerConnectionFactory.java) and [JNI factory network-monitor selection](https://webrtc.googlesource.com/src/+/refs/heads/main/sdk/android/src/jni/pc/peer_connection_factory.cc). These explain the public disableNetworkMonitor option and its Android monitor factory effect; the build also verifies availability in the pinned SDK.
